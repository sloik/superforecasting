# Tetlock's Ten Commandments for Aspiring Superforecasters

> **This is a paraphrased summary with our own commentary and tool notes — not the book's
> text.** The ten headings below restate, in our words, the structure of the Appendix to
> *Superforecasting: The Art and Science of Prediction* (Tetlock & Gardner, 2015). For the
> original wording and the reasoning behind each one, read the book.

These are behaviours **experimentally shown** to raise forecasting accuracy in the Good
Judgment Project. Guidelines, not laws (see the meta-commandment).

1. **Triage.** Focus effort where it pays. Skip questions that are trivially easy
   (*clocklike* — a rule of thumb suffices) or effectively impossible (*cloudlike* — even
   good models can't beat chance). Work the Goldilocks zone in between.

2. **Break seemingly intractable problems into tractable sub-problems (Fermi-ize).**
   Decompose. Flush ignorance into the open. Separate the knowable from the unknowable and
   estimate the parts.

3. **Strike the right balance between inside and outside views.**
   Start with the **outside view** — the base rate: "How often do things of this sort happen
   in situations of this sort?" — then adjust with the inside (case-specific) view. Searching
   for a comparison class, even for "unique" events, is the habit that separates the best.

4. **Strike the right balance between under- and over-reacting to evidence.**
   Update often but in small, proportionate steps. Tease signal from noisy news; resist both
   anchoring (under-reaction) and chasing headlines (over-reaction). Belief updating is to
   forecasting what flossing is to dental hygiene.

5. **Look for the clashing causal forces at work in each problem.**
   For every argument, find the counterargument worth taking seriously. Be a fox: synthesize
   many causal drivers rather than forcing one Big Idea.

6. **Strive to distinguish as many degrees of doubt as the problem permits, but no more.**
   Use fine-grained probabilities (0.63, not "likely") when warranted; avoid false precision
   when it isn't. Rounding a superforecaster's probabilities coarsens them and degrades accuracy.

7. **Strike the right balance between under- and over-confidence, prudence and decisiveness.**
   Long-run accuracy needs both good **calibration** (your 70%s happen ~70% of the time) and
   good **resolution** (you dare to move away from 50%).

8. **Look for the errors behind your mistakes but beware of rearview-mirror hindsight biases.**
   Do honest post-mortems. Don't rewrite history so you were "almost right"; don't over-learn
   from a single outcome that luck could explain.

9. **Bring out the best in others and let others bring out the best in you.**
   Precision questioning, constructive confrontation, sharing rationales. Teams of
   superforecasters beat individuals — aggregate diverse, independent views.

10. **Master the error-balancing bicycle.**
    Skill comes from *doing* with prompt, accurate feedback — deliberate practice — not from
    reading about it. Keep riding: act, get scored, adjust.

**Meta-commandment: don't treat the commandments as commandments.**
Von Moltke: "It is impossible to lay down binding rules, because two cases will never be
exactly the same." Judgment over checklists.
